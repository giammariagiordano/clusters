
Cluster 0: "Steady Contributors"
- **Traits**: Moderate followers (~270), about 76 public repositories, average GitHub experience (~10 years).
- **Behavior**: Active but not explosive; steady commit and issue activity, moderate insertions and deletions.
- **Motivation for grouping**: This group embodies consistent contributors — not celebrities, but regular coders pushing the community forward.
- **Reasoning**: Mid-range metrics across followers, commits, insertions, and repository counts.

Cluster 1: "Heavyweight Developers"
- **Traits**: High followers (~780), large commit counts (~1800), very high code insertions/deletions.
- **Behavior**: Professional-level contribution volumes, deep involvement across multiple projects.
- **Motivation for grouping**: The statistical peaks in bugs solved, features implemented, and task resolution sharply distinguished them.
- **Reasoning**: Their very high activity, issue tracking, and heavy repo participation make them 'industry players.'

Cluster 2: "Elite Open-Source Maintainers"
- **Traits**: Astronomical stars (avg ~11,000 stars), very high forks (1500+), low commit frequency.
- **Behavior**: Focused more on project ownership and maintenance rather than day-to-day coding.
- **Motivation for grouping**: Their extreme popularity metrics (stars and forks) despite fewer commits made them unique.
- **Reasoning**: They represent top-tier repository owners whose projects impact large developer communities.

Cluster 3: "Legacy Powerhouses"
- **Traits**: Very high commit counts (~2800), massive insertions/deletions (~5 million lines changed), moderate followers (~1800).
- **Behavior**: Veterans with long-standing contributions across large codebases.
- **Motivation for grouping**: Their longevity and massive codebase involvement with a moderate online following are distinct.
- **Reasoning**: Although they don't chase followers or stars aggressively, their code contributions define their reputation.
